# 3. Literature Review

## I. Purpose and Method

The purpose of this literature review is to examine research on the social adaptation of care leavers, mentoring practices, digital mentoring platforms, and computational methods for mentor–mentee matching. The review focuses on evidence relevant to developing a web-based mentoring and social adaptation platform for orphanage residents and graduates of boarding schools in Kazakhstan.

The literature was selected according to its relevance to the project's functional and technical requirements. The sources include empirical studies of young people in care, research on mentoring and social inclusion, computational matching studies, and recent research on large language models (LLMs) in recommender systems. Foundational publications were retained where they provide important methodological contributions, while recent research was included to examine emerging AI-based recommendation approaches.

Fourteen sources are included in the comparative analysis. These comprise scientific journal articles, conference proceedings, and one organizational report. The review compares research methods, findings, limitations, and implications for the proposed platform. Particular attention is given to how evidence can inform matching criteria, secure communication, life-skills development, recommendation transparency, and coordinator supervision.

## III. Critical Review

### 3.1 Social Adaptation and Mentoring

Research on care leavers indicates that the transition to independent living involves interconnected social, educational, and practical challenges. The Kazakhstan study by Sadyrova and Simtikov provides local evidence on socialisation and life-skills development, while Kaasinen et al. examine social inclusion among Finnish care leavers [3], [7]. These findings support designing the platform around individualized goals rather than limiting it to the initial introduction between a mentor and a mentee.

Research on natural mentoring also suggests that participation in activities can be associated with access to supportive adults [1]. This provides a rationale for including interests and preferred activities in mentee profiles. However, shared interests alone should not determine compatibility because a suitable mentoring relationship may also depend on experience, goals, availability, and the needs of each participant.

Digital mentoring research provides further guidance for the platform's functional requirements. Radlick et al. examine the experiences and needs of young people and mentors in multicultural mentoring contexts, offering insights into digital support and relationship-building [9]. Research on socio-educational interventions also emphasizes individualized support during the transition to adulthood [12]. Together, these sources inform the proposed use of goal tracking, structured mentoring activities, and coordinator monitoring.

### 3.2 Computational Matching and Recommendation Systems

Traditional matching approaches provide a practical starting point for the platform's recommendation module. Haas et al. investigate two-sided matching, where the preferences of both mentors and mentees are considered [2]. Varghese and Mohaghegh propose a hybrid machine-learning approach that combines collaborative filtering with content-based filtering [8]. These studies demonstrate different ways of structuring compatibility criteria, although neither establishes which method would work best for young people leaving institutional care in Kazakhstan.

Reciprocal recommendation research also highlights the importance of considering both parties' preferences rather than optimizing only for the mentee's stated needs [10]. Nevertheless, mentor–mentee matching differs from contexts such as online dating because the platform must prioritize mentor screening, safeguarding, eligibility, and coordinator oversight. Therefore, recommendation scores should be used to support decisions rather than automatically approve relationships.

### 3.3 LLM-Based Recommendation and Natural-Language Profiles

Recent research on LLM-enhanced recommender systems provides an additional technical direction for interpreting open-ended profile responses. Lin et al. review how LLMs can be integrated into different stages of recommendation systems, including feature representation and scoring or ranking [13]. This provides a conceptual basis for investigating language-based representations of mentees' goals and mentors' skills.

Ramos et al. explore natural-language user profiles that make recommendation preferences more transparent and easier to edit [14]. Their findings are relevant to a platform in which young people describe their interests and goals in their own words. However, their study concerns item recommendations, not mentoring, so its approach would need to be adapted and evaluated in the proposed context.

For this project, a practical implementation could use a pretrained sentence-embedding model to represent open-ended profile descriptions and calculate semantic similarity between mentee goals and mentor experience. A weighted matching algorithm could then combine this similarity with structured criteria such as interests, location, language, and availability. This design is a proposed application of existing recommendation techniques, not a claim that the cited LLM studies have already validated mentor–mentee matching.

### 3.4 Research Gap and Implications for Our Platform

The reviewed literature covers social adaptation, mentoring, computational matching, and LLM-enhanced recommendation, but these areas are not equally represented. Studies of care leavers emphasize social inclusion and practical support, whereas technical matching studies generally investigate other populations or recommendation settings [7], [8]. The LLM studies provide methods for text-based recommendation and transparent profiles, but do not directly evaluate mentoring platforms for orphanage residents in Kazakhstan [13], [14].

The identified gap is therefore the need to investigate how established matching approaches and language-based recommendation techniques can be adapted to a supervised mentoring and social adaptation platform in the Kazakhstan context. This is a focused project gap rather than a claim that no comparable systems exist.

The literature informs the following design decisions:

| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

The proposed contribution is the implementation and evaluation of a web platform that combines AI-assisted mentor recommendations with structured social adaptation support. The system will use profile information to suggest potentially suitable mentors, while coordinators retain responsibility for approving matches. Recommendation quality will be assessed against a rule-based baseline using an appropriate test dataset. If representative real-world matching data are unavailable, synthetic or carefully constructed test cases will be clearly identified, and conclusions will be limited accordingly.

## IV. Limitations of This Review

This review has several limitations. First, the studies differ in populations, research methods, and national contexts, so findings from higher education, online dating, or other youth-support settings cannot be directly generalized to care leavers in Kazakhstan. Second, the technical literature on LLM-enhanced recommendation is broader than the specific mentoring problem; therefore, its methods provide a foundation for investigation rather than direct evidence of improved mentoring outcomes.

Third, the proposed matching approach has not yet been validated on a representative dataset of mentors and mentees from the target population. The usefulness of sentence embeddings, the selection of weighted criteria, and the quality of recommendations must therefore be established empirically. Finally, the bibliographic details of references [5], [6], and [11] require confirmation against their original publications or reports before the review is submitted.
