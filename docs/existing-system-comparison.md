## Existing System Comparison

Several established programs support mentoring, social adaptation, and independent living for vulnerable young people. The comparison includes a Kazakhstani program and international examples. Their public websites document the services described below; where a feature is not documented, it is marked as **not publicly documented** rather than assumed to be unavailable.

### 2. Main Findings and Identified Gap

The comparison shows that mentoring and social adaptation programs already operate in Kazakhstan and internationally. DARA Foundation is particularly relevant because its Mentors project supports teenagers from orphanages through trained adult volunteers, educational activities, and career guidance. Its online training platform demonstrates that digital tools are already used in the local mentoring context [1]–[2].

International programs demonstrate additional possibilities. Silver Lining Mentoring provides online communication, profile-based matching, goal-oriented support, and staff monitoring through its e-mentoring platform [3]. Therefore, these capabilities should not be presented as entirely new or unavailable in existing systems.

The proposed **Mentoring and Social Adaptation Platform** addresses a more specific need: adapting and integrating relevant digital workflows for the Kazakhstani context. Its planned features include:

- Mentor applications and coordinator-managed screening.
- Interest-, location-, and availability-based matching recommendations, with the final decision made by a coordinator.
- Secure, coordinator-visible communication and incident reporting.
- Meeting records, individual goals, and progress monitoring.
- Practical life-skills resources for housing, budgeting, documentation, education, and employment.
- Continued mentoring and social adaptation support for graduates transitioning to independent living.
- Future development of machine-learning-based matching, evaluated against a simpler baseline.

The intended contribution is **not the claim that no mentoring programs or digital tools exist**. It is the development of a locally adapted, integrated platform that brings these workflows together for children in residential care and graduates of boarding schools in Kazakhstan.

The system must also account for unequal access to personal smartphones and reliable internet. Online tools should support, rather than replace, in-person mentoring and coordinator assistance.
